THE COST OF INACTION

What’s at Stake: Testimony, People & Resources

In an unpredictable world, proactive stewardship of safety and security is not optional — it is faithful leadership.

Risks to Testimony

High-profile incidents damage public perception and may diminish your ability to minister to your congregation and community.

  • • Media scrutiny and long-term reputational harm
  • • Legal battles that drain focus and finances
  • • Erosion of trust when the vulnerable are not protected

Risks to People

Your congregation likely spans from independent and agile to dependent and vulnerable. Physical threats, medical emergencies, and failures in child protection can cause profound and lasting harm.

Research tracking incidents at U.S. houses of worship (2000–2024) documents hundreds of violent events and nearly 500 deaths. While mass attacks are statistically rare, the consequences are devastating when they occur.
  • • Physical harm or loss of life
  • • Trauma to survivors, families, and the body of Christ
  • • Long-term spiritual damage from betrayal of trust

Risks to Resources

Incidents may create immediate and long-term financial strain through lawsuits, insurance increases, property damage, cyber attacks, and declining attendance and giving.

Rising Threat

Ransomware and data extortion attacks on churches and faith-based organizations are increasing. Recovery costs frequently exceed ransoms, and many general liability policies have significant gaps in security-related coverage.

  • • Costly litigation and potential uninsured liability
  • • Operational disruption and lost revenue
  • • Leadership time diverted to crisis response
A CLEAR PATH FORWARD

How to Start

Protecting your church doesn’t require a massive overhaul. It begins with a deliberate, step-by-step process.

01

Learn the threats and risks

Understand the range of challenges facing churches — physical, cyber, reputational, and legal.

02

Identify particular threats to your church

Every congregation has unique vulnerabilities based on location, size, ministries, facility layout, and community context.

03

Assess existing capabilities

Perform an objective inventory of current policies, training, procedures, team structure, and physical measures already in place.

04

Address gaps

Prioritize and address the most likely and most damaging vulnerabilities with realistic, scalable, and biblically grounded solutions.

05

Continuously evaluate and adjust

A healthy safety and security ministry matures with your church through regular reviews, training, and after-action learning.

THE BEST TIME IS BEFORE YOU NEED IT

When?

Now.

Disasters, accidents, and threats don't follow a schedule. The best time to strengthen these systems is before you need them — exactly like maintaining the roof, HVAC, or plumbing.

A DELIBERATE, PROVEN APPROACH

How to Implement & Sustain

Ad-hoc measures and “security theater” create false confidence. Lasting protection requires clear intent, solid policies, trained people, and ongoing discipline.

Implementation Framework

1
Develop Overall Intent

A theologically grounded Safety & Security Ministry Intent Statement approved by your leadership that serves as the north star for every decision.

2
Establish Supporting Policies

Clear, written policies covering child protection, disruption response, facility access, cybersecurity, medical emergencies, and incident reporting.

3
Build Organization & Procedures

Establish or formalize an existing Safety & Security Team with defined roles, documented procedures, role-specific training, drills, and OODA-informed decision making.

Sustaining the Ministry

Leadership Investment

Visible support from senior leadership and governing board, with realistic budget and time allocation.

Regular Reviews and Learning

Annual risk assessments, quarterly team meetings, and disciplined after-action reviews after drills or incidents.

Cultural Integration

Safety awareness woven into volunteer onboarding, leadership development, and occasional church-wide emphasis.

Documentation & Succession

Document everything so knowledge becomes accessible and transferable. Develop clear succession planning for key roles.
